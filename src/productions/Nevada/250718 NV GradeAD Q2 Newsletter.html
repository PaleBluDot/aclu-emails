Q2 Newsletter
 

While the federal government rolls back critical civil rights and civil liberties, we are more committed than ever to safeguarding civil rights for Nevadans. From the streets to the courts, our team has been working nonstop to protect our rights and empower communities. Here's a snapshot of what we accomplished together this spring:

Protest Rights Hotline


As protests began to take root across the country, we launched our On The Ground volunteer program to make sure Nevadans can exercise their First Amendment rights safely and confidently. Our ground team volunteers and partners conducted legal observations and shared reliable Know Your Rights information with protesters in Las Vegas, Reno, and Carson City, and our civil rights attorneys launched a new Protest Rights Hotline.

Protecting Due Process


In Viloria Aviles v. Trump, our civil rights attorneys blocked the administration's attempt to deport a man to El Salvador without due process under the Alien Enemies Act. While other cases related to this kind of deportation action are ongoing, our team will keep fighting to make sure government processes give every Nevadan a fair shake.

Nevada Calls on Congress

The Nevada Legislature approved Assembly Joint Resolution 9 this spring, making Nevada the first state in the country to formally urge Congress to protect schools and places of worship from ICE raids.

Por La Gente, For the People

Our Por La Gente community canvass program launched with a simple but powerful mission: Make sure our immigrant communities have access to reliable Know Your Rights information. Dedicated volunteers across the state have distributed more than 500 Know Your Rights posters to local businesses to equip employees and employers with the information they need to stay ready.

If you or someone you know may want a poster for their Nevada business, you can request one on our website.

Defending Students' First Amendment Rights

As graduation season approached, we filed a lawsuit against Clark County School District, challenging its inconsistent and unconstitutional restrictions on how students could decorate their caps and gowns. After an emergency court hearing, the court ruled in our favor, and dozens of students were able to celebrate this once-in-a-lifetime moment free from censorship.

None of this work happens without you. Whether you joined a protest, spent an afternoon volunteering, or made a donation to fund our work, you've been a part of every win and every change we've made.
