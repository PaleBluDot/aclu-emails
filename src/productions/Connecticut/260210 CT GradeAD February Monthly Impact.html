LIVE UPDATE: We're In Court Today!

As you are reading this newsletter, our legal team is in court defending our First Amendment right to free speech and protest in Quinn v. Eucalitto.

This case stems from a series of recent encounters that our clients have faced, which have led to dispersal orders, arrests, and prosecutions of peaceful demonstrators. State police have insisted that sign-holders are trespassing or illegally displaying signs that may distract drivers on I-95. The ACLU Foundation of Connecticut's lawsuit contends that the state is unconstitutionally forbidding people on public streets and sidewalks to display protest signs, and that overpass sidewalks are traditional public fora where peaceful expressive activity is protected.

Learn more about Quinn v. Eucalitto on our website.

We're Suing…Again

As you may have seen, the U.S. Department of Justice (DOJ) has requested voter data and other election information from several states over the summer, with plans to request data from all 50 states eventually. In Connecticut, the demands for complete voter registration data for every registered voter in the state include sensitive and private information such as the Social Security and driver's license numbers – we will not allow the federal government to intimidate eligible voters in Connecticut and across the country.

In January 2026, the ACLU Foundation of Connecticut intervened in United States v. Thomas, alongside Common Cause and one individual, to protect the privacy of Connecticut residents and to ensure that our sensitive personal information is not misused to intimidate or disqualify any eligible voter from exercising their most fundamental right.

Read more about United States v. Thomas on our website.

Legislative Session Has Started, And We've Got Big Plans

The 2026 legislative session is a short one, running from February to May. During a shorter session and escalating federal threats, the ACLU of Connecticut is advancing a legislative agenda designed to protect core civil liberties while remaining flexible enough for rapid response.

Our priorities are defending against federal overreach, collaborating in deep alignment with community organizers and coalition partners to advance collective legal strategy and public education efforts, and building sustainable power among our movement to respond to the moment we are in.

Read about our legislative priorities on our website.

Together, We Found Power and Purpose!

In January, we held a training institute with our colleagues at ACLU National, engaging with almost one hundred Connecticut residents ready to take their power and mobilize for a collective purpose. At the Power & Purpose Training Institute, folks are left with tangible actions and tools to organize their community, have persuasive conversations that move people to action, and build lasting connections with others who are ready to rise to the moment.

Thank you to all the folks who committed to not only coming to the event but also engaged deeply with the program and our staff. We are eager to see you all again at future events!
