Spring Newsletter
 

We're back with another update on the happenings at the ACLU of Maine! In this edition: a quick look at where key bills stand in the Maine Legislature, why our legal team is investigating ICE activities in Maine, and a preview of upcoming community events across the state.

It's been a busy couple of months in Augusta, as the legislature addresses several ACLU of Maine priorities. A quick recap of what our policy team has been up to at the State House:

  • Data Privacy: We're another step closer to passing one of the strongest privacy bills in the nation. LD 1822 would limit the amount of personal information companies can collect about us, and would safeguard our most sensitive data. It won a committee vote in late May and we're working to get it passed by the full House and Senate.
  • Upholding the Right to a Speedy Trial: The Constitution guarantees the right to a speedy trial, and Maine is one of seven states without clear timelines to uphold this right. LD 340 would help by setting clear timelines in law and allowing courts and prosecutors to prioritize resources. The bill passed out of committee and is heading to the full House and Senate.
  • Defending Due Process and Community Safety: LD 1656, a bill that would require Maine to facilitate federal immigration enforcement, lost a committee vote. LD 1971, an immigration bill that defends due process, preserves local resources, and strengthens public safety, will receive a committee vote soon.
  • There is still more to come at the State House this session. Follow our bill tracker and check out Weekly Highlights to stay in the loop on the latest developments!

In April, our legal team began demanding information from government agencies to uncover how the President's "mass deportation" efforts are being carried out here in Maine by local law enforcement as well as by ICE and Customs and Border Patrol (CBP).

We sent Freedom of Access Act (FOAA) requests to every county in Maine asking for basic information about each sheriff's office relationship with ICE and CBP. We also sent FOAA requests to Wells, Monmouth, and Winthrop – Maine towns that were considering official contracts with ICE known as 287 (g) agreements."

We've learned that three Maine jails have contracts with the U.S. Marshals service to incarcerate people on behalf of ICE. At least one other jail is detaining people for ICE, seemingly without a contract with the federal government. Additionally, Wells, Monmouth, and Winthrop have all paused or rescinded their 287 (g) contracts with ICE following significant community opposition.

We'll continue to request information to help us develop a clear understanding of how federal immigration enforcement is being pursued in Maine and whether these practices are violating civil rights and liberties in Maine. Read more about our open records requests.
