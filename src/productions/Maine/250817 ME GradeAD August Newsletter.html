A note from the executive director

As we move through a pivotal year for civil rights and liberties, I want to take a moment to provide some updates about all that we've accomplished together so far in 2025.

At the State House, our policy team wrapped up a busy legislative session, working tirelessly with coalition partners to safeguard the rights of Mainers – from the right to a fair trial and bodily autonomy, to free speech and voting rights.

In court, we've made major progress in two lawsuits challenging unconstitutional executive orders by President Trump. We're also continuing our fight in Robbins to ensure Maine fulfills its Sixth Amendment obligation to provide attorneys to people who cannot afford their own.

Out in the community, it's been an honor to connect with supporters and partners across Maine. Since welcoming our engagement and education coordinator in March, we've taken part in 34 events across the state, from Machias to Bangor to Kennebunk. We've had the joy of meeting both new and longtime supporters and engaging in meaningful conversations about the future of civil liberties in Maine and how we can show up for your communities.

None of this would be possible without you. Your support and continued involvement fuels our work every day. Keep reading to catch up on highlights from the past few weeks.

There have been important developments in three of our major cases in recent months. Our legal team continues to fight for Mainers' Sixth Amendment right to an attorney, and we'll be at Maine's supreme court this fall to keep it moving forward. We also recently secured key victories in two of our lawsuits against the president's illegal executive orders: one protecting birthright citizenship, and another upholding First Amendment free speech rights.

  • Robbins v. State of Maine: In March, the Kennebec County Superior Court ruled in our favor, finding that Maine is violating the Sixth Amendment by failing to provide attorneys to people charged with a crime. In June, the state appealed the decision, which paused any relief for people currently jailed without legal representation. Oral arguments in the appeal are scheduled for early October before the Maine Supreme Judicial Court.
  • Barbara v. Donald J. Trump: A federal court in New Hampshire blocked Trump's executive order restricting birthright citizenship and certified a nationwide class that protects the citizenship of all children born on U.S. soil. This is our second case to protect birthright citizenship.

  • Smith v. Trump: In a major win for the First Amendment, a federal court in Maine blocked enforcement of an executive order that would punish U.S. human rights advocates for speaking to the International Criminal Court. The court agreed that the order violates free speech rights. One of our clients is from Maine and investigates gender-based violence in Afghanistan. This executive order prevented him from exercising his freedom of speech to share information, evidence, legal advice, and expertise with the ICC.

The Maine State Legislature has adjourned for the year!

Our policy team spent hundreds of hours in Augusta testifying on bills, meeting with lawmakers, and working alongside partners to advocate for Mainers' civil rights and liberties. The legislative session unfolded against a backdrop of rising political tension and legal uncertainty, giving us critical opportunities to defend civil liberties across six core areas: the right to a fair trial, data privacy, due process, bodily autonomy, free speech, and voting rights.

Some highlights:

  • Along with our coalition partners, we defeated all eight anti-abortion and anti-trans bills this session, sending a message to lawmakers that Mainers believe in bodily autonomy. Abortion and gender-affirming care both remain legal in Maine.
  • We championed a bill to fund new public defender positions, helping to strengthen Maine's ability to uphold the constitutional right to a fair trial. This legislative action is another key part of ending this crisis, right alongside our litigation in Robbins v. Maine.
  • We helped defeat all efforts to restrict voting rights, including mandatory voter ID and ending absentee voting. However, a referendum question coming to Maine voters in the November 4, 2025, election takes aim at Maine's safe and secure voting system, including absentee voting. This referendum would make it harder for people across the state to vote – especially seniors, Mainers with a disability, and Mainers without reliable transportation. We're part of the campaign to stop Question 1. Learn more at SaveAbsentee.me and vote no on Question 1.
