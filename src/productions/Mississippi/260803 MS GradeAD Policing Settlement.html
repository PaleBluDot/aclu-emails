Today, the ACLU of Mississippi announced a settlement in four lawsuits against the City of Lexington, brought on behalf of people who alleged that Lexington police officers arrested them unlawfully, used excessive force, fabricated charges against them, and held them for days without due process. We litigated these cases alongside our pro bono co-counsel at Willkie Farr & Gallagher LLP, O'Melveny & Myers, and Morgan, Lewis & Bockius LLP.

Our clients share their names and their words. "I was thrown to the ground and jailed for days simply for trying to get a court-ordered reimbursement from Lexington Police," said Deborah Powell. "I'm happy that this settlement provides me some justice." Andrial Young said, "I feel that Lexington Police mistreated me badly. I hope this settlement helps Lexington improve its policing in the future." Javarius Russell, a law enforcement officer himself, said, "I believe Lexington Police violated my rights. I'm pleased to get some justice for myself and others with this settlement.

These cases were part of our Police Accountability Initiative, which enlists law firms, private attorneys, and community organizations to bring lawsuits that stop police misconduct. The initiative puts serious legal resources behind people who face a police department with lawyers of its own. The litigation also drew on findings from a 2024 U.S. Department of Justice investigation that concluded the Lexington Police Department had engaged in unconstitutional and racially discriminatory policing.

There was no admission or finding of liability or wrongdoing by the defendants as part of this settlement. Our Legal Director Joshua Tom said police must balance enforcing the law with protecting constitutional rights, and that this settlement helps achieve that balance for the department, our clients, and the residents of Lexington.

Police accountability work continues across Mississippi. Read the full announcement, and support the work that made this settlement possible.
