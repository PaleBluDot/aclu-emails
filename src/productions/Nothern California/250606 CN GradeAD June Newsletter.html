June Newsletter
 

Welcome to the first issue of The Brief, the monthly email newsletter of the ACLU of Northern California. Here, you'll find timely updates on our work and opportunities to get involved. And since it's hard to keep up with everything that's happening, we'll also highlight important news you may have missed. We're in the fight of our lives to save our democracy. We hope you'll speak out, show up, and stand with us.

Defending humanitarian relief for Venezuelans and Haitians


The U.S. Supreme Court has allowed the Trump administration to cancel Temporary Protected Status (TPS) for 350,000 Venezuelan immigrants while our case challenging the termination of humanitarian relief for more than one million Venezuelan and Haitian TPS holders proceeds. The court's decision left the group in limbo. While they face the prospect of deportation, the government hasn't indicated when it might act. However, last week, a federal judge preserved protections for 5,000 Venezuelans who already received renewed documents allowing them to live and work in the U.S. under the January TPS extension. The next hearing is in July. We remain committed to fighting for the rights of all TPS holders.

Protecting campus free speech


With the Trump administration targeting students for pro-Palestine activism, it's critical that universities protect free speech rights on campus. This spring, we scored key victories – for privacy and the right to protest – against UC Santa Cruz.

A judge restricted UCSC police access to data on a student protester's phone after we argued that an overbroad search warrant violated the California Electronic Communications Privacy Act. The original warrant gave campus police access to privileged messages between the student and their attorneys about their case against the school.

And because of our lawsuit on behalf of students and faculty who were arrested and banned from campus for protesting, UCSC was forced to follow strict rules and provide due process before banishing someone and cutting them off from housing, classes, jobs, and healthcare.

Count every vote


California sends every active registered voter a mail ballot. Under state law, all mail ballots postmarked by Election Day are counted if they arrive within a seven-day grace period.

If San Diego Congressman Darrell Issa's lawsuit seeking to prevent the state from counting mail ballots received after Election Day succeeds, hundreds of thousands of California voters could be disenfranchised. Not on our watch. The ACLUs of California represent the League of Women Voters of California, which is urging the court to block this flagrantly partisan attack on voting rights.

Before Stonewall


In honor of Pride Month, we're digging into the ACLU News archives to bring you a little-known story about a 1956 police raid at a San Mateo County gay bar and a riot a decade later at a Tenderloin diner popular with the trans community.

Prosecutors charged 87 Hazel's Inn patrons with vagrancy. Of the 30 men ACLU NorCal attorneys defended, two were found guilty of “lewd and dissolute” behavior and banned from the county for two years.
